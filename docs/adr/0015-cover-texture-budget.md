# Covers are resized to 512px, under a per-cover cap and a total budget

Staged covers are resized to 512px, and the build enforces both a per-file cap and a `TEXTURE_BUDGET_BYTES` ceiling for the library as a whole. A cover already inside the cap is copied byte for byte rather than re-encoded.

The shelf is WebGL: every cover is decoded into an _uncompressed_ GPU texture and all of them are uploaded before the first frame. Thirty-one covers were 8.4 MB on the wire and **314 MB decoded** — the desktop absorbed it and a phone killed the tab. Two numbers rather than one, because what dies is the sum, and the sum grows with the library.

## How this was decided

_Carried verbatim from the Decision Log this repository kept from July 2026, newest last._

- **2026-08-01** — **Covers are resized to 512px when staged, because the shelf is WebGL and a JPEG is not what a phone has to hold.** The vault keeps whatever the provider gave — Apple's artwork runs to 2400px — and that is right for the vault; it is your copy of the cover. It was wrong for the shelf, where every cover is decoded into an _uncompressed_ GPU texture and all of them are uploaded before the first frame. Thirty-one covers were 8.4 MB on the wire and **314 MB decoded**. The desktop absorbed it; a phone killed the tab, and reloading hit a browser that then refused to hand out a context at all. Found by the owner on a phone, not by any gate — see `docs/gates.md`, row G15. A cover already inside the cap is copied byte for byte rather than re-encoded, so nothing is degraded that did not need touching.

- **2026-08-01** — **Two numbers, not one: a per-cover cap and a total budget.** Capping each file alone only moves the crash further down the shelf, because what actually dies is the sum, and the sum grows with the library. `TEXTURE_BUDGET_BYTES` is therefore a ceiling the library grows _into_ — at 512px it holds somewhere north of eighty books, and the brief's 200-book target does not fit and is not meant to. It is expected to go red one day on a build that changed nothing, which is the point: on a machine rather than on someone's phone. The fix at that point is lazy or on-demand texture upload. Raising the number would make it a comment.

- **2026-08-01** — **The covers were not the cause, and the fix for them was still right.** Reproduced on multiple phones in private tabs with the compressed covers confirmed arriving, so 314 MB → 30 MB was necessary and not sufficient. The tempting next move — tune pixel ratio, antialiasing and the 2048² shadow map, all of which were deferred — would be tuning against an unidentified cause, so two instruments went in first. **`?debug` writes a black box to `localStorage` once a second**, because a renderer OOM kill destroys the process: there is no exception, nothing reaches `onerror`, and a USB session disconnects at the moment the data matters, so a console can show a clean log followed by nothing. `pagehide` fires on an ordinary navigation and not on a kill, which makes **a stored record with no `clean` flag a record of a crash** — that asymmetry is the entire diagnosis. **`?books=N` bisects on the device that actually crashes**: five books dying means the fixed cost is the problem and library size is irrelevant, five surviving and twenty-five not means it is cumulative and N is the threshold. Both paths observed in both directions before shipping. Nothing is written to a visitor's device without the parameter, and the panel exposes only renderer counters and the GPU string — no vault data.

- **2026-08-01** — **A failed shelf says so instead of showing an empty room.** `mountShelf` throws when the browser will not grant a WebGL context, and `boot()`'s rejection went nowhere — the `.astro` script may not catch it under the "no logic in `.astro`" rule — so the second symptom the owner reported ("when I refresh the shelf doesn't load") was an unhandled promise and a blank page. `boot` now catches and renders a sentence. `webglcontextlost` is also handled, and `preventDefault` is the whole of what makes a restore possible: the browser's default is that the context is gone for good and `webglcontextrestored` never fires.
