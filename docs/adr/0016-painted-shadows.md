# Shadows are painted, not rasterised

The shelf computes its shading once, from the same layout the books were placed with, and draws it to a canvas as one textured plane per shelf. There is no shadow pass, no depth target and no per-fragment lookup. `?shadows=1` keeps the real-time path for hardware that can hold it.

Nothing in this scene moves — books are placed at mount, the light never moves, and a shadow does not depend on the camera, the only thing that does. Real-time shadow mapping is a technique for scenes that change. It is also the only configuration that survives on the hardware below, and the investigation that established that is the substance of this ADR.

## How this was decided

_Carried verbatim from the Decision Log this repository kept from July 2026, newest last._

- **2026-07-31** — **Deferred to the Phase 2 aesthetics review:** real covers often yield desaturated spine colours (a live `stacks add` gave `#d6d6d5` for a genuinely pale cover). The extractor is not wrong, but a shelf of grey spines may read badly. Do not tune this blind — decide it against a screenshot.

- **2026-08-01** — **Deferred, again against a screenshot rather than blind:** device-pixel-ratio, shadow-map size and antialiasing are each single-digit MB against the 314 that caused the crash, and every one of them changes what `pnpm smoke:render` screenshots. Tuning them in the same change would have meant debugging the gate instead of the bug. *(Superseded: the reasoning above is dead. The 314 MB is gone and the shelf still loses its context with **five books, 632 triangles and eleven textures**, so the cost that matters is the fixed one and these three are no longer single-digit against anything. They are now probes — see the entry below.)*

- **2026-08-01** — **The bisect answered on the first reload, and it ruled out the fix that was about to be built.** `?debug&books=5` on the owner's Pixel 10 Pro lost the context after 12 seconds with 632 triangles, 61 draw calls and 11 textures — so the cost that kills the shelf is paid before a book is drawn, library size is not the variable, and a lazy cover loader would have been an elaborate fix for a cause that is not this one. **It is also not the tab being killed**: the reload reported `previous session ended cleanly`, which requires `pagehide` to have fired, which requires the page to have lived. The context went away underneath a surviving document. The original sad-face report was a *tab* death and is a different failure; the 314 MB fix plausibly did resolve that one, and this was underneath it all along. Device: PowerVR D-Series DXT-48-1536 — the Tensor G5's Imagination GPU, whose driver is new — with a 1054×1926 buffer at dpr 2.

- **2026-08-01** — **Four separate probes, not one "mobile profile".** A bundle of *antialias off, lower dpr, no shadows, guarded resize* would very likely make the crash disappear and leave nobody able to say which knob did it — then ship three permanent quality regressions to fix one bug. So `?aa=0`, `?dpr=N`, `?shadows=0` and `?guard=1` are independent, ranked by what the numbers implicate: 4× MSAA colour+depth at 1054×1926 is ~65 MB and the largest allocation in the scene, on a tile-based GPU where the resolve is the expensive path; dpr sets the size everything else multiplies; the shadow map is 16 MB; the resize guard is last but stays live because the failure is *delayed* (12s, 19s) rather than at first paint, and assigning `canvas.width` reallocates the drawing buffer even when the value is unchanged. **Each was verified to have a real effect before shipping** — `aa=0` flips `gl.getContextAttributes().antialias`, `dpr=1` takes the buffer from 1000×1800 to 500×900, `shadows=0` drops the renderer from 13 textures and 3 programs to 11 and 2 — because a probe that silently does nothing is worse than no probe: it would be run, show no change, and clear the actual cause. The panel prints the active profile, since a bisect that cannot be tied to a configuration is an anecdote. Whichever probe holds becomes the default, alone.

- **2026-08-01** — **It was the shadow pass, and the ranking that predicted otherwise was wrong.** Thirty-one books with antialiasing on and pixel ratio 2, shadows off: stable. Shadows on: dead in 6–18 seconds. One variable. Antialiasing was ranked first on the arithmetic — 4× MSAA colour and depth at 1054×1926 is ~65 MB and *is* still the largest allocation in the scene — and the shelf runs fine with it. The 2048² shadow map is 16 MB, was ranked third, and is the one that takes the context. **Sizing an allocation predicted the wrong answer**, which is the whole argument for probing over reasoning about it, and the reason each knob shipped separately instead of as a profile: a bundle would have "worked" and taught nothing.

- **2026-08-01** — **Shadows stay on by default anyway — owner's call, and aesthetics are the owner's.** Defaulting them off was the obvious move from that result and was rejected: shadows are most of what makes the case read as furniture rather than as coloured boxes, and a shelf without them is not the shelf. `smoke:render` measures the difference at identical 25.5% coverage with distinct colours 1305 → 1202, and it is visible. So the question is not whether to keep them but **which cheaper form of them a phone can hold**. Defaulting them off *for phones* would be worse than either: "mobile" is not detectable in any way that stays true, and it would create a rule about visitors that nothing here could check — the same shape as the Cloudflare zone setting no gate can see. Whatever survives becomes the default for everyone.

- **2026-08-01** — **A static scene must not redraw its shadow map every frame, and this one was.** Nothing on the shelf moves: books are placed at mount, the light never moves, and a directional light's shadow map is a function of the light and the geometry and *not* of the camera — which is the only thing that does move. So a full extra pass ran sixty times a second to produce an image identical to the last one. `shadowMap.autoUpdate = false` plus one `needsUpdate` ends it, and the measurement is unambiguous: on the 49-book fixture, steady-state draws are **302 with shadows on and 302 with shadows off**, where the shadow pass previously added ~196. The restore path needs its own `needsUpdate` — the one-shot map dies with the context, and without it a restored shelf renders shadowless and silently. Two further reductions came with it: **one caster per book instead of four**, since a book's shadow is its outline and the page block already gives that outline to within the binder's square (~3mm on a 230mm book, under half a texel); and **a shadow camera actually fitted to the case**, which it never was — `DirectionalLight` aims at the origin through a fixed ±5 box, the case stands *on* the origin and grows upward, so a 5.6-tall unit was half outside its own shadow frustum and the top of a tall shelf fell out of it. Fitting it covers ~7 units instead of 10 with the same map, so `smoke:render` reports distinct colours 1305 → **1318**: more detail, not less. **This removes the pass cost and not the other two candidates** — the depth target is still allocated and still sampled per fragment by PCFSoft — so if the driver's problem was either of those it is unfixed, and the probes stay.

- **2026-08-01** — **Shadows are painted, not rasterised, because a static scene should never have been shadow-mapping in the first place.** The probes ran out: on the Pixel 10 Pro every real-time configuration lost the context — `soft@2048`, `basic@2048`, `basic@512`, and `casters=0` where nothing is drawn into the map at all — while `shadows=off` ran 118 seconds to a clean exit. All of them render the same frame: 195 draws, 1,720 triangles, 7–10 MB of heap on a phone with 8 GB. **It is not a load**, and it was never a memory problem; the *first* bug was, and carrying that frame into a second unrelated failure cost most of a day. The GPU is Imagination's PowerVR D-Series, which the Tensor G5 took up in place of ARM Mali, so silicon and driver are both months old. Nothing here can fix a driver; not depending on it is the fix. Three's shadows are a technique for scenes that *change* — render from the light into a depth texture, sample it per fragment — and nothing on this shelf does: books are placed once, the light never moves, and a shadow does not depend on the camera, the only thing that does. So `contact-shadow.ts` computes the shading once from the same layout the books were placed with and draws it to a canvas — a soft body and a tighter root under each book, plus the ambient darkening where a shelf meets its backboard — as one textured plane per shelf. No pass, no depth target, no per-fragment lookup. `?shadows=1` keeps the real-time path for hardware that can hold it. It also gains something real-time never gave: **the corner darkening reaches empty shelves**, so the bottom of a growing case stops reading as different furniture from the top. Limits, stated: contact shadows only — books do not shade each other and nothing falls on the backboard — and `ctx.filter` is feature-checked by write-and-read-back, because without it the same code paints hard black rectangles under every book, which is worse than no shadow; there, the corner darkening stays and the books' own shadows are skipped.

- **2026-08-01** — **`?casters=0` is the only shadow probe that discriminates, and it is the one to run first.** `?shadowmap=N` and `?shadowtype=basic|pcf|soft` both just make the shadow work smaller, so surviving either says only "less was cheaper" and leaves the axis unknown. `casters=0` keeps the depth target allocated and the pass running over an *empty* scene, which separates the two candidate mechanisms outright: if the shelf lives, the cost is drawing ~190 casters and thinning them fixes it with the shadows intact — the page block is sealed inside the case and cannot cast anything visible, so a quarter of them are pure waste. If it still dies, the cost is the target or the shader sampling it, no amount of thinning helps, and the answer is **baked contact shadows** — the scene is static, so that shading can be painted into the wood rather than computed every frame, which keeps the look on every device and costs nothing at runtime.

- **2026-08-01** — **`LOST_MESSAGE` no longer asserts a cause.** It said the shelf "ran out of graphics memory"; `webglcontextlost` carries no reason, and the evidence then contradicted the guess — the page survived and exited cleanly, so nothing ran out of anything it could name. Copy shown to a visitor is a claim like any other.

- **2026-08-01** — **A face-out book's footprint was the size of its cover.** Found by the owner, in a screenshot. Turned a quarter turn, such a book puts `coverWidth` across the plank and only its own `thickness` deep — but both dimensions were taken from the cover, so a shadow the size of the whole cover lay flat on the wood and reached most of the way to the shelf's front edge. A dark smudge standing in front of a book, thrown by a light that is in front of it. The class of bug a still render hides and a person spots at once.

- **2026-08-01** — **The real-time path is not fixable on this device: the shader will not link.** Remote debugging with unminified sources produced what six bisect configurations could not — `THREE.WebGLProgram: Shader Error 0`, empty program log, empty shader logs, then `useProgram: program not valid` sixty times a second until the context dies. `soft@2048`, `basic@2048`, `basic@512` and `casters=0` all died alike because **none of them change whether a program links**; it was never a budget. The failing material is a `MeshBasicMaterial` — a painted shadow plane, unlit, wanting nothing to do with shadows — and three's `meshbasic` shader includes no shadow chunk in either stage, so enabling `shadowMap` adds two inert `#define`s to that program and it stops linking. Also: **`VALIDATE_STATUS false` in that message means nothing**, because three prints the parameter without ever calling `gl.validateProgram` and its initial value is `false`. It reads like a second finding and is not one.

- **2026-08-01** — **`PCFSoftShadowMap` has been gone since before the probes that thought they were testing it.** three 0.185 deprecated it and substitutes `PCFShadowMap`, with a console warning nobody had read — so `?shadowtype=soft` and `?shadowtype=pcf` were the same renderer, and the diagnostics panel was naming a filter that was not running. Mapped honestly rather than dropped, so an old URL still works and reports what it actually got. A probe that silently does nothing is worse than no probe; so is one that silently does something else.

- **2026-08-01** — **`dispose()` never freed a painted shadow's texture, and the comment saying it did was load-bearing.** The scene walk disposed `map` only for `MeshStandardMaterial`, so every shadow — all of them unlit `MeshBasicMaterial` — leaked its canvas, and the count had just grown by two per shelf. Worse, `makeNeighbourShadow` cited that walk as the reason it may not share a texture between books. Both kinds are now named; covers are disposed twice, once here and once by the cache, which is free because `dispose()` is idempotent and cheaper than working out which is which.

- **2026-08-01** — **The shelf stops when a shader will not link, because otherwise the instrument dies with the page.** Three logs the failed link and carries on: `useProgram` on the invalid program every frame, `INVALID_OPERATION` every frame, context gone in a second or two — which is exactly why nothing about this failure has ever been readable. `renderer.debug.onShaderError` now halts the render loop on the first failure and shows a sentence. One bad frame is enough to know; sixty a second only destroys the evidence. **Observed in both directions** by forcing `LINK_STATUS` false in the browser: with the failure `requestAnimationFrame` is scheduled once and stays at one, without it 1119 → 1600 over two seconds. Deliberately not a *fallback* — silently swapping to painted shadows would turn a clean "this device cannot link the shader" into "shows something, for reasons you would have to dig for", and the recovery path could not be observed failing on the hardware that matters.

- **2026-08-01** — **The handler asks the driver the question three never asks.** Three prints `VALIDATE_STATUS` without ever calling `gl.validateProgram`, so the `false` in its error message is that parameter's *initial value* and carries no information — it reads like a second symptom and is not one. The handler calls `validateProgram` and reads the validate log, which is a different log from the link log, plus both shader logs, `getError`, and the limits that usually explain a program that compiles and will not link: varying vectors, uniform vectors, texture units. All of it lands in the black box, so it survives the session and can be read off a phone with no cable.

- **2026-08-01** — **`?painted=0` is the probe that discriminates, and it also makes `?shadows=1` mean something again.** The painted shadows are the only `MeshBasicMaterial` in the scene, so leaving them out drops the program count 3 → 2 and removes the material that will not link: if `?painted=0&shadows=1` runs on the Pixel, the fault is specific to those programs and there is something to change; if it still fails, the lit materials fail too and there is not. Everything else tried — map size, filter, `casters=0` — only made the shadow work smaller, which is the same mistake as the first round of probes. Second job: the two shadow systems are independent, so `?shadows=1` has always drawn real shadows *on top of* the painted ones and double-darkened everything they agree about. Every reference screenshot taken that way is painted+real, not real.

- **2026-08-01** — **Installing `onShaderError` silences three's own message, so the handler has to say more than what it replaced.** The callback sits in the `else` branch of the link-status test, which means the report got *quieter* as it got better — the console line the whole diagnosis had been read from simply stopped appearing. It now logs its own, and recovers the one genuinely useful thing three prints and the callback is not passed: the material. That survives in the shader source, because every program three builds is prefixed with `#define SHADER_TYPE` and `#define SHADER_NAME`. The generic context-lost notice is also suppressed once a shader has failed — the loss follows a moment later on this hardware, and the vague sentence was landing on top of the specific one.

- **2026-08-01** — **An adversarial leak audit found no monotonic leak on any repeating path — and one defect in the instrument added to find the crash.** The render loop allocates nothing (OrbitControls uses module-scope temporaries unless `zoomToCursor` is on, which it is not), the shadow pass costs nothing after the first frame, the picker reuses its raycaster, and `TextureCache` is bounded by the library. What it did find was mine: `shaderErrors` was uncapped while `errors` beside it is capped at eight, and `halted` only stops the *next* frame — three returns from the callback and keeps walking the render list, so one frame can fail several programs, each pushing nine lines into an array that `diagnostics.ts` serialises into `localStorage` every second forever. A black box that grows its own writes on a device already in trouble is the one thing it must not do. Now only the first report is kept, with a line saying more followed; the console still gets every one. Observed bounded: two failures, ten lines, 655 stored bytes and unchanged three seconds later.

- **2026-08-01** — **`dispose()` never freed a geometry, and the two shared unit shapes are why it must not simply free them all.** Every book is a scaled `UNIT_BOX` or `UNIT_PLANE` held at module scope, so a blanket `geometry.dispose()` in the teardown walk would free them for the whole module and leave a second mount drawing nothing. Named exceptions instead. Latent either way — nothing calls `dispose()` today — but a teardown that does not tear down is exactly the drift this project gates against, and it becomes real the moment anything remounts.

- **2026-08-01** — **The shadow pass is exonerated, and `receiveShadow` cannot help.** `autoUpdate = false` means the pass runs exactly once, at first paint, so it cannot take a context away twelve seconds later — and `casters=0` dying agrees, since that only removes draws from *inside* that one pass. What is left is what persists: a render target three allocates as a 2048² depth texture **plus** a 2048² RGBA8 colour texture it never samples (16 MB each, half of it for nothing), and a per-fragment depth fetch in every lit material, every frame, forever. The obvious next idea — stop the books receiving shadows — would change nothing: three keys `USE_SHADOWMAP` on `shadowMap.enabled` and the light count alone, never on the mesh. So `?shadowfetch=0` draws the map once and then turns the flag off and recompiles: the target stays allocated, the map stays drawn, the sampling stops. Survives → the fetch is the killer. Dies → holding a sampled depth attachment is, and there is nothing left to fix. Verified to do what it claims: programs 4 → 7 at the second frame with textures unchanged at 80, so the materials really were rebuilt and the target really was kept.

- **2026-08-01** — **An empty case still dies.** `?shadows=1&books=0&painted=0` — no books, no painted planes, just the wood — loses the context. Slower than a full shelf, which is itself informative, but it goes. Nothing about the library, the covers, the spine textures or the painted shading is involved in this at all, and no amount of thinning the scene is a fix.

- **2026-08-01** — **It is the depth *fetch*, and that finally points at a fix rather than at a wall.** `?shadowfetch=0` survives on the Pixel: draw the shadow map once, keep the 32 MB render target allocated, stop every material reading it, and the context holds. So the allocation is fine and the one-shot pass is fine — what kills the driver is sampling the depth texture per fragment. That also explains the shape of the whole bisect: `basic`, `pcf` and `soft` all declare `uniform sampler2DShadow` and read the map with a *hardware depth comparison*, so every one of them exercises the same broken operation and every one died alike at every size and filter. `?shadowtype=vsm` is the only configuration in three that does not: variance shadow maps store depth and depth-squared in an ordinary texture and read it back with a plain `sampler2D`. Untested on the device, renders correctly on the desktop at +1 texture, +1 geometry and +2 programs for the blur. Note the trade if it holds: under VSM every shadow *receiver* also casts, so the one-caster-per-book economy stops applying.

- **2026-08-01** — **VSM dies too, and that closes it: nothing that reads a shadow map survives on this device.** Variance shadow maps were the last candidate — the only path in three that reads the map with a plain `sampler2D` rather than the `sampler2DShadow` hardware comparison the other three share — so the fault is not the comparison sampler either. Put beside the two results before it, the bisect is complete: an **empty case** dies, so nothing about the library or the shading is involved; `?shadowfetch=0` **survives** with the whole 32 MB target still allocated and the map still drawn, so neither the allocation nor the one-shot pass is the problem. What every dying configuration has and the survivor does not is materials that read the map at all. Stated honestly, that probe does not separate *sampling* from *binding* — turning the flag off also stops three uploading the shadow uniforms and binding those textures each frame — and nothing available here can separate them; it does not matter, because the conclusion is the same either way. **The painted shading is therefore not a workaround but the only design that works on this hardware**, and its case never depended on the crash: a scene where nothing moves should not have been shadow-mapping to begin with. `?shadows=1` stays for hardware that can hold it. Investigation closed.

- **2026-08-01** — **Nobody has published this exact symptom, and the hardware's reputation is the corroboration.** Searched: there is no report anywhere of a Pixel 10, a Tensor G5 or a PowerVR D-Series losing a WebGL context on a shadow map. What is documented is everything around it — the Pixel 10 shipped with Imagination driver **v24.3** while **v25.1** already existed, its GPU sat at its 396 MHz idle clock under load against a rated 1 GHz, games showed flickering textures and screen tearing, and Google acknowledged it and shipped driver fixes in monthly patches without committing to v25.1. Depth-texture sampling bugs are a recurring class rather than a freak: Firefox carried one that reproduced on NVIDIA under macOS and nowhere else, and AMD shipped a driver with `sampler2DShadow` shadow mapping broken outright. So the finding is consistent with the public record without being confirmed by it, which is the honest way to hold it. **It may simply come right with a system update**, which is part of why `?shadows=1` is kept — nothing here can detect that, so re-testing needs a person and a phone, the same thing that found it.
