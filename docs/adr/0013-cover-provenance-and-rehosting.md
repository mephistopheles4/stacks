# Cover provenance comes from the bytes, and all of it is re-hosted knowingly

`cover_source` records which provider a cover's bytes came from, taken from the URL actually downloaded — not from whichever provider answered the metadata lookup, because the two routinely differ and it is the bytes whose terms apply. Absent and `unknown` mean different things, and an unrecognised value is dropped rather than kept.

The deployed shelf re-hosts every cover it has, honouring takedown requests. That is a decision made against a measurement — 25 Apple, 6 Open Library, 0 Google — which showed the strict "Open Library only" policy would have cost twenty-five covers and kept six.

## How this was decided

_Carried verbatim from the Decision Log this repository kept from July 2026, newest last._

- **2026-07-31** — **`cover_source` is derived from the URL downloaded, not from the provider that answered the lookup.** The metadata layer completes one provider's record from another's and consults Apple purely for artwork, so the two routinely differ — and it is the bytes whose terms apply. Parsed through an allowlist: an unrecognised value is dropped rather than kept, because a typo must not read as a permission.

- **2026-07-31** — **Provenance is recorded now; the provider policy is not enforced yet.** Every cover cached before `cover_source` existed has none, and the owner's real vault is all of them, so dropping unattributed covers would empty the shelf. Record going forward, decide the backfill, then enforce.

- **2026-08-01** — **Measured the vault before deciding the policy, and the measurement decided it.** 31 covers: **25 Apple, 6 Open Library, 0 Google**. Every Open Library cover is exactly 500px tall — the cap on its `-L.jpg` — and everything else is 778–2400px wide, which is the Apple rewrite. Google contributed nothing, which is consistent with Apple having been added to replace its ~128px thumbnails. So "re-host Open Library only" would have meant six covers and twenty-five generated spines, trading away exactly the cover quality Apple was added for. The strict policy was affordable in the abstract and not in this vault.

- **2026-08-01** — **Re-hosting all cover art on the deployed site, knowingly, and honouring takedowns.** Owner's call, made against the numbers above. Google's terms bar permanent copies of API content and Apple conditions promotional content on a store badge linking to a purchase page — so this is outside both, for a personal non-commercial shelf where the realistic worst case is a takedown request, which the terms themselves make the remedy. Recorded here rather than left implicit, because an undocumented decision is indistinguishable from an oversight. The alternative that keeps the art *and* satisfies Apple — a "View on Apple Books" link from `trackViewUrl` on the detail card — stays available if that ever matters.

- **2026-08-01** — **`stacks covers --backfill` infers provenance from image dimensions, and says so.** A command rather than a one-off script: anyone who used this tool before `cover_source` existed has the same vault full of unattributed covers, and a script nobody can re-run or review is barely better than doing it by hand. It only writes a key that is absent, so an observation recorded at download time always beats the guess; it writes `unknown` when the shape is not diagnostic, because "looked and could not tell" is a different and truer statement than the key being missing; and it prints that the values were inferred, since that fact will outlive anyone's memory of the run.

- **2026-08-01** — **The case shades itself; the books were never doing it.** The owner named "the shelf casts a shadow on top of all books" as the thing the painted shelf was missing. It does not, and nothing else does either — every book stands its front within 2cm of the case's front plane, so a ray leaving a cover escapes into the room before anything can block it. What is dark is the **backboard**, which is the full depth of the case back: the plank's shadow falls about three quarters of the way down it, and read as a picture that is a dark band across the top of every shelf. So three painters, each **one plane per row** rather than per book — a backboard shade, a recess falloff under the plank and at both uprights, and the right upright's real wedge across the plank. The two cast ones are computed from the key light's actual position (`caseLight` in `scene.ts`) rather than tuned, so moving the light cannot leave them describing where it used to be; the recess deliberately is not, because a cast shadow from an upright would fall on one side only and barely touch a book, while the corner is dark on both sides whatever the light does. **Strengths were set by differencing against the real thing** — `?shadows=1` still renders the shadow map, so the two can be photographed at one camera and subtracted, and the first pass came back measurably darker than what it was imitating. Cost +10 draws, +10 textures, +20 triangles at 31 books; `smoke:render` distinct colours 1165 → **1285**, more tonal detail rather than less.
